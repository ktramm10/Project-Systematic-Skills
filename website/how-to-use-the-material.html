How to Use the Material:

Here are some tips on how to effectively use the instructional material provided on this site:

  • Start with the fundamentals: Make sure you have a solid understanding of the basic techniques before moving on to more advanced concepts. I recommend starting with the foundations of effective BJJ series to build an effective understanding of the fundamental movements needed to be successful long term.
  • Pace your learning: When working through the material, take your time and work through section by section. After completing a section take the time to drill the techniques try them on lower level training partners in sparring. Trying to funnel to much information into your game at once can leave you with only a surface level understanding of the material. Your goal should be to integrate the techniques into your game gradually immersing yourself in a particular technique or set of techniques.
  • Practice regularly: Consistent practice is essential for skill development. Set aside dedicated time for training. In almost all gyms across the globe, there is time to practice your own techniques both before and after class.
  • Find like minded training partners: Look for others who share your goals and commitment to improvement. These will be the people you go to for drilling and feedback. Even though Jiu Jitsu is a individual sport, having good training partners is a requirement for success.
  • Review and revisit: It is uncommon to understand all the important details of a position or techique in one sitting. Don't hesitate to go back and review previous material to reinforce your understanding and retention.
  • Ask questions: If you have any doubts or need clarification, feel free to reach out to me either in the academy or through the contact information provided.

Example Usage:

Here is an example of how to apply the principles discussed:

  1. Complete a video section: Start by watching a complete video section and taking notes on the key points.
  2. Drill the techniques you learned with a good training partner: Find a training partner who you trust to help you practice and refine the techniques. Some training partners will try to overly resist and defeat your techniques the first time you attempt them. This is not what we are looking for, the first time you try the technique it should be with zero resistence focus entirely on understanding the core body movements required to execute it properly. After you have good understanding of the details needed to make the techniques effective. Try them with limited resistance and work your way up to full sparing intensity over time identifying problems and reviewing the instructional footage along the way.
  3. Attempt the techniques against low level opponents in the training room When you first learn a new technique, regardless of your overall skill level in the sport, with that technique you are a beginner. Don't make the mistake of trying to use it against experienced high level opponents and becoming discourage when it is shut down. Start with lower level players in the room and refine your technique over time elevating the skill level of your training partners as you improve.
  4. When you hit a wall, reach out for help: Feel free to reach out to me either in the academy or through the contact information provided. With that being said, feel free to ask your fellow training partners or your instructor for guidance.
